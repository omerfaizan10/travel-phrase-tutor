# Spanish at a restaurant, café or tapas bar

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Spain" (1984), Module IV "Dining Out" (café counter and restaurant dialogues, exercises, keys) and the companion "Cultural Notes" (food, meals, beverages), https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-spanish-headstart-for-spain-course/. US Government work, public domain. Also: Wikivoyage, "Spanish phrasebook" (Eating, Bars), https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Spanish_phrasebook, and "Spain" (Eat section), https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Spain. CC BY-SA 4.0. Prices in the FSI dialogues were in pesetas (pre-2002) and are left out; Spain now uses the euro. Pronunciations follow Spain (z, ce, ci = "th").

## Getting a table and the menu
Country: ES (Spain)

- Una mesa para dos personas, por favor. [OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah dohs pehr-SOH-nahs, pohr fah-BOHR] = A table for two, please.
- Una mesa para una persona, por favor. [OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah OO-nah pehr-SOH-nah, pohr fah-BOHR] = A table for one, please.
- Me gustaría reservar una mesa para esta noche. [meh goos-tah-REE-ah reh-sehr-BAR OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah EHS-tah NOH-cheh] = I'd like to reserve a table for tonight.
- ¿Puedo mirar la carta, por favor? [PWEH-doh mee-RAR lah KAR-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Can I look at the menu, please? (Spain says la carta. In Latin America: ¿Puedo ver el menú?)
- ¡Camarero! [kah-mah-REH-roh] = Waiter! (Spain. Mesero in Latin America, mozo in Argentina and Uruguay.)
- ¿Cuál es el plato del día? [kwahl ehs ehl PLAH-toh dehl DEE-ah] = What is the special of the day?
- ¿Cuál es la sopa del día? [kwahl ehs lah SOH-pah dehl DEE-ah] = What is the soup of the day?
- ¿Cuál es la especialidad de este restaurante? [kwahl ehs lah ehs-peh-thyah-lee-DAHD deh EHS-teh rehs-tow-RAHN-teh] = What is the specialty of this restaurant?
- ¿Hay alguna especialidad de la casa? [AY ahl-GOO-nah ehs-peh-thyah-lee-DAHD deh lah KAH-sah] = Is there a house specialty?
- ¿Qué es cocido? [keh ehs koh-THEE-doh] = What is cocido? (Ask "¿Qué es ...?" about any dish.)

## What the waiter says
Country: ES (Spain)

- (waiter) ¿Qué van a tomar? [keh bahn ah toh-MAR] = What are you going to have? (to several people)
- (waiter) ¿Qué va a tomar? [keh bah ah toh-MAR] = What are you going to have? (to one person)
- (waiter) ¿Qué van a pedir? [keh bahn ah peh-DEER] = What are you going to order?
- (waiter) ¿Qué va a comer? [keh bah ah koh-MEHR] = What are you going to eat?
- (waiter) ¿Qué va a ser? [keh bah ah sehr] = What will it be?
- (waiter) ¿Y de principio, qué desean? [ee deh preen-THEE-pyoh, keh deh-SEH-ahn] = And for the main course, what would you like?
- (waiter) ¿Qué van a beber? [keh bahn ah beh-BEHR] = What are you going to drink?
- (waiter) ¿Con gas o sin gas? [kohn gahs oh seen gahs] = Sparkling or still? (about mineral water)
- (waiter) ¿Cómo quiere el filete? [KOH-moh KYEH-reh ehl fee-LEH-teh] = How do you want your steak?
- (waiter) ¿Postre? [POHS-treh] = Dessert?
- (waiter) ¿Más tapas? [mahs TAH-pahs] = More tapas?
- (waiter) Enseguida. [ehn-seh-GEE-dah] = Right away.

## Ordering food
Country: ES (Spain)

- Tráigame ..., por favor. [TRAY-gah-meh ..., pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring me ..., please. (used when ordering at a table)
- Tráiganos ..., por favor. [TRAY-gah-nohs ..., pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring us ..., please.
- Póngame ... [POHN-gah-meh] = Give me ... (used when ordering at the bar counter)
- Quisiera ... [kee-SYEH-rah] = I would like ... (polite)
- Para mí, paella. [PAH-rah MEE, pah-EH-yah] = For me, paella.
- Unos entremeses para la señora y cocido para mí. [OO-nohs ehn-treh-MEH-sehs PAH-rah lah seh-NYOH-rah ee koh-THEE-doh PAH-rah MEE] = Some appetizers for the lady and cocido for me.
- Merluza frita para la señora y para mí un filete a la plancha con alcachofas. [mehr-LOO-thah FREE-tah PAH-rah lah seh-NYOH-rah ee PAH-rah MEE oon fee-LEH-teh ah lah PLAHN-chah kohn ahl-kah-CHOH-fahs] = Fried hake for the lady, and for me grilled steak with artichokes.
- Quisiera la sopa del día. [kee-SYEH-rah lah SOH-pah dehl DEE-ah] = I would like the soup of the day.
- Tráigame un filete a la plancha, medio asado, por favor. [TRAY-gah-meh oon fee-LEH-teh ah lah PLAHN-chah, MEH-dyoh ah-SAH-doh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring me a grilled steak, medium, please.
- Quisiera el filete bien asado. [kee-SYEH-rah ehl fee-LEH-teh byehn ah-SAH-doh] = I would like the steak well done.
- Quisiera un plato que lleve pollo. [kee-SYEH-rah oon PLAH-toh keh YEH-beh POH-yoh] = I want a dish containing chicken.

## Ordering drinks
Country: ES (Spain)

- Yo, una caña. [yoh, OO-nah KAH-nyah] = For me, a small draught beer. (Una caña is the usual way to order beer in a Spanish bar, about 200 ml.)
- Un chato. [oon CHAH-toh] = A small glass of wine.
- Tráigame una cerveza, por favor. [TRAY-gah-meh OO-nah thehr-BEH-thah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring me a beer, please.
- Tráiganos dos chatos, por favor. [TRAY-gah-nohs dohs CHAH-tohs, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring us two glasses of wine, please.
- Tráigame una botella de vino tinto, por favor. [TRAY-gah-meh OO-nah boh-TEH-yah deh BEE-noh TEEN-toh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring me a bottle of red wine, please.
- Tráiganos media botella de vino blanco. [TRAY-gah-nohs MEH-dyah boh-TEH-yah deh BEE-noh BLAHN-koh] = Bring us half a bottle of white wine.
## More drinks, coffee and toasts
Country: ES (Spain)

- Traiga una jarra de sangría. [TRY-gah OO-nah HAH-rrah deh sahn-GREE-ah] = Bring a pitcher of sangría.
- Traiga un vaso de agua, por favor. [TRY-gah oon BAH-soh deh AH-gwah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring a glass of water, please.
- Agua mineral sin gas. [AH-gwah mee-neh-RAHL seen gahs] = Still mineral water.
- Quisiera café solo. [kee-SYEH-rah kah-FEH SOH-loh] = I would like a black coffee.
- Tráigame una taza de café. [TRAY-gah-meh OO-nah TAH-thah deh kah-FEH] = Bring me a cup of coffee.
- Otra caña, por favor. [OH-trah KAH-nyah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Another beer, please.
- Otra ronda, por favor. [OH-trah ROHN-dah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Another round, please.
- ¡Salud! [sah-LOOD] = Cheers!
- vino tinto = red wine; vino blanco = white wine; clarete = rosé; agua mineral = mineral water; leche = milk; té = tea; zumo de naranja = orange juice (Spain; jugo in Latin America)
  Coffee in Spain: café solo = black, cortado = with a dash of milk, café con leche = white coffee. Asking for "un chocolate" in a café means hot chocolate.

## Tapas at the bar
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Qué tapas hay? [keh TAH-pahs AY] = What tapas are there?
- (waiter) Las que ustedes ven. También hay patatas fritas, chorizo, boquerones ... [lahs keh oos-TEH-dehs behn. tahm-BYEHN AY pah-TAH-tahs FREE-tahs, choh-REE-thoh, boh-keh-ROH-nehs] = The ones you see. There are also crisps, chorizo, boquerones ...
- Póngame aceitunas y boquerones. [POHN-gah-meh ah-thay-TOO-nahs ee boh-keh-ROH-nehs] = Give me olives and boquerones.
- Una ración de chipirones y otra de gambas. [OO-nah rah-THYOHN deh chee-pee-ROH-nehs ee OH-trah deh GAHM-bahs] = A portion of small squid and another of prawns.
- ¿Tiene algo para picar? [TYEH-neh AHL-goh PAH-rah pee-KAR] = Do you have any bar snacks?
- patatas fritas = crisps / fried potatoes; chorizo = spicy red sausage; boquerones = small pickled or fried fish; jamón serrano = cured country ham; gambas = shrimp / prawns; aceitunas = olives; almejas = clams; mejillones = mussels; chipirones = small squid
  A tapa is a small snack served with a drink. A larger portion is una ración. Going from bar to bar for tapas is ir de tapeo.

## Saying how it is and paying
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Cómo está la paella? [KOH-moh ehs-TAH lah pah-EH-yah] = How is the paella?
- Está muy rica. [ehs-TAH MOO-ee REE-kah] = It's very tasty.
- Está muy bueno. [ehs-TAH MOO-ee BWEH-noh] = It's very good.
- Estaba delicioso. [ehs-TAH-bah deh-lee-THYOH-soh] = It was delicious.
- He acabado. [eh ah-kah-BAH-doh] = I'm finished.
- La cuenta, por favor. [lah KWEHN-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = The bill, please.
- Tráigame la cuenta, por favor. [TRAY-gah-meh lah KWEHN-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Bring me the bill, please.
- (waiter) Son ... euros. [sohn ... EW-rohs] = That's ... euros.
- Aquí tiene. Quédese con la vuelta. [ah-KEE TYEH-neh. KEH-deh-seh kohn lah BWEHL-tah] = Here you are. Keep the change.
- ¿Aceptan tarjeta de crédito? [ah-THEHP-tahn tar-HEH-tah deh KREH-dee-toh] = Do you accept credit cards?
  In Spain you must ask for the bill; the waiter will not bring it until you do.

## Spanish dishes on the menu
Country: ES (Spain)

- cocido = a Madrid stew of chickpeas, meat, chicken, blood sausage (morcilla), chorizo, potatoes and cabbage
- paella = Valencian rice dish with meat, chicken, fish or seafood and vegetables
- gazpacho = cold Andalusian soup of tomatoes, bread, garlic, vinegar, oil and water
- tortilla de patatas = potato omelette
- callos a la madrileña = tripe, Madrid style, in a somewhat spicy sauce
- bacalao a la vizcaína = salt cod, Bilbao (Basque) style, with tomatoes
- merluza frita = fried hake (a very popular fish all year)
- pimientos rellenos = stuffed peppers; chuletas de cerdo = pork chops; pollo en cacerola = stewed chicken; lenguado frito = fried sole
- ensaladilla = salad of cooked vegetables with mayonnaise; sopa de pescado = fish soup; caldo de gallina = chicken broth
- fabada = Asturian white bean stew with salt pork and sausage
- queso manchego = Manchego cheese from La Mancha
- flan = custard; helado de vainilla / de fresa = vanilla / strawberry ice cream; arroz con leche = rice pudding; natillas = custard; fruta = fruit

## Meal times and customs in Spain
Country: ES (Spain)

- el desayuno = breakfast (light: coffee and a pastry)
- la comida = lunch, the main meal (in Latin America often el almuerzo)
- la cena = dinner
- el menú del día = fixed-price lunch menu (often a bargain, water or wine usually included)
- el vino de la casa = the house wine (served from a pitcher, cheaper than bottled)
- agua del grifo = tap water (free, but not every restaurant offers it)
  Lunch is usually between about 1:30 and 4 pm. Dinner starts at 8:30 or 9 pm; in Madrid few restaurants open before 9 pm. Between lunch and dinner many restaurants are closed, but a bar will usually have a bocadillo (baguette sandwich).

## Restaurant conversation step by step (Libro Libre)
Country: ES (Spain)

Source: Erin Huebener, "Libro Libre: Beginning Spanish" (2018), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (this section is shared under the same licence), "En el restaurante" (p. 172). English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- (waiter) ¿Cuántos son? [KWAHN-tohs sohn] = How many are you?
- Somos cinco. [SOH-mohs THEEN-koh] = There are five of us.
- (waiter) ¿Desean algo para beber? [deh-SEH-ahn AHL-goh PAH-rah beh-BEHR] = Would you like something to drink?
- Para beber, me gustaría agua, por favor. [PAH-rah beh-BEHR, meh goos-tah-REE-ah AH-gwah, pohr fah-BOHR] = To drink, I'd like water, please.
- (waiter) ¿Están listos para pedir? [ehs-TAHN LEES-tohs PAH-rah peh-DEER] = Are you ready to order?
- Necesitamos más tiempo para decidir. [neh-theh-see-TAH-mohs mahs TYEHM-poh PAH-rah deh-thee-DEER] = We need more time to decide.
- Sí, estamos listos. [see, ehs-TAH-mohs LEES-tohs] = Yes, we're ready.
- (waiter) ¿Qué desean comer? [keh deh-SEH-ahn koh-MEHR] = What would you like to eat?
- ¿Qué nos recomienda? [keh nohs reh-koh-MYEHN-dah] = What do you recommend?
- Quisiera el plato del día. [kee-SYEH-rah ehl PLAH-toh dehl DEE-ah] = I'd like the dish of the day.
- La cuenta, por favor. [lah KWEHN-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = The bill, please.
- (waiter) Enseguida. [ehn-seh-GHEE-dah] = Right away.

## Menú del día and restaurant manners in Spain (Libro Libre)
Country: ES (Spain)

Source: Erin Huebener, "Libro Libre: Beginning Spanish" (2018), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (this section is shared under the same licence), pp. 172-173. Summarised in our words.

- Menú del día = set menu of the day: you choose a primero (first course) and a segundo (second course); depending on the restaurant it includes postre (dessert), bebida (drink) or pan (bread). An affordable multi-course meal.
- Example menú del día (8.50 euros on the photographed board): primeros = sopa de pescado (fish soup), ensalada mixta (mixed salad), guisantes con jamón (peas with ham); segundos = lomo a la plancha (grilled pork loin), filete de ternera (veal steak), pollo al chilindrón (chicken in pepper sauce); postres caseros = flan, natillas (custard), tarta de Santiago (almond cake), fruta fresca (fresh fruit).
- The server usually checks on you once after the food arrives and then leaves you alone. That is good manners, not poor service.
- You must ask for the bill (La cuenta, por favor): bringing it unasked would feel like being told to leave.

## At the restaurant: ordering and paying (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module IV Unit 1 "Ordering Drinks" and Unit 2 "Ordering the Meal" (pp. 186-188, 198-200); set in Panama, https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- (waiter) Por aquí, señores. [pohr ah-KEE, seh-NYOH-rehs] = This way. (señores = sir and ma'am, used even when women are in the group)
- El menú, por favor. [ehl meh-NOO, pohr fah-BOHR] = The menu, please.
- (waiter) Sí, cómo no. En un momentito. [see, KOH-moh noh. ehn oon moh-mehn-TEE-toh] = Sure. Just a second.
- (waiter) Aquí tienen el menú. ¿Van a tomar algo, señores? [ah-KEE TYEH-nehn ehl meh-NOO. bahn ah toh-MAHR AHL-goh, seh-NYOH-rehs] = Here's the menu. Are you going to have something to drink?
- Yo no voy a tomar nada. [yoh noh boy ah toh-MAHR NAH-dah] = I'm not going to drink anything.
- (waiter) ¿Qué desean comer primero? [keh deh-SEH-ahn koh-MEHR pree-MEH-roh] = What would you like to eat first?
- Yo quiero un ceviche de corvina. [yoh KYEH-roh oon seh-BEE-cheh deh kohr-BEE-nah] = I'd like a corvina ceviche.
- ¿Cuál es la sopa de la casa? [kwahl ehs lah SOH-pah deh lah KAH-sah] = What's the soup of the day?
- (waiter) Hoy tenemos sancocho. [oy teh-NEH-mohs sahn-KOH-choh] = Today we have sancocho.
- ¿Qué es eso? [keh ehs EH-soh] = What's that?
- (waiter) Es una sopa de pollo con yuca, plátano verde y muchas especias. [ehs OO-nah SOH-pah deh POH-yoh kohn YOO-kah, PLAH-tah-noh BEHR-deh ee MOO-chahs ehs-PEH-syahs] = It's chicken soup with yucca, green plantain and lots of spices.
- Deme sancocho y langosta en mantequilla. [DEH-meh sahn-KOH-choh ee lahn-GOHS-tah ehn mahn-teh-KEE-yah] = Give me sancocho and lobster in butter.
- (waiter) ¿Y de tomar, señores? [ee deh toh-MAHR, seh-NYOH-rehs] = And to drink?
- (waiter) ¿Van a querer postre? [bahn ah keh-REHR POHS-treh] = Are you going to want dessert?
- Yo no quiero postre. Solamente tráigame una taza de café y la cuenta, por favor. [yoh noh KYEH-roh POHS-treh. soh-lah-MEHN-teh TRY-gah-meh OO-nah TAH-sah deh kah-FEH ee lah KWEHN-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = I don't want dessert. Just bring me a cup of coffee and the check, please.
- ¿Dónde queda el servicio? [DOHN-deh KEH-dah ehl sehr-BEE-syoh] = Where is the restroom?
  The course's Panama notes (1980s) say service can be slower than in the United States, so be patient. A tip of 10 percent was standard in moderately priced restaurants and 10 to 20 percent in expensive ones. Check current customs. In this course the waiter is called camarero.

## At a restaurant (Puerto Rico)
Country: PR (Puerto Rico)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Puerto Rico", Module IV Unit 1 "Ordering Drinks" (conversation and notes pp. 177-178) and Unit 2 "Ordering the Meal" (conversation and notes pp. 187-189), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-puerto-rico. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- Viajero: Buenas noches. Una mesa para dos personas. [BWEH-nahs NOH-chehs. OO-nah MEH-sah PAH-rah dohs pehr-SOH-nahs] = Good evening. A table for two.
- Mesero: Por aquí, señores. [pohr ah-KEE, seh-NYOH-rehs] = This way, sir and madam.
- Viajero: El menú, por favor. [ehl meh-NOO, pohr fah-BOHR] = The menu, please.
- Mesero: Sí, cómo no. En un momentito. [SEE, KOH-moh noh. ehn oon moh-mehn-TEE-toh] = Yes, of course. In just a moment.
- Mesero: Aquí tienen el menú. ¿Van a tomar algo, señores? [ah-KEE TYEH-nehn ehl meh-NOO. BAHN ah toh-MAR AHL-goh, seh-NYOH-rehs] = Here's the menu. Are you going to have something to drink?
- Viajera: Yo no voy a tomar nada, gracias. [yoh noh BOY ah toh-MAR NAH-dah, GRAH-syahs] = I'm not going to have anything, thank you.
- Mesero: ¿Desean un aperitivo? [deh-SEH-ahn oon ah-peh-ree-TEE-boh] = Would you like an appetizer?
- Viajera: Yo quiero un coctel de camarones. [yoh KYEH-roh oon kohk-TEHL deh kah-mah-ROH-nehs] = I'd like a shrimp cocktail.
- Mesero: ¿Qué van a pedir, señores? [KEH BAHN ah peh-DEER, seh-NYOH-rehs] = What are you going to order?
- Viajera: Para mí, ensalada de espárragos y arroz con pollo, por favor. [PAH-rah MEE, ehn-sah-LAH-dah deh ehs-PAH-rrah-gohs ee ah-RROHS kohn POH-yoh, pohr fah-BOHR] = For me, asparagus salad and chicken and rice, please.
- Mesero: Hoy tenemos caldo gallego. [OY teh-NEH-mohs KAHL-doh gah-YEH-goh] = Today we have caldo gallego (a vegetable soup with many spices).
- Viajero: ¿Qué es eso? [KEH ehs EH-soh] = What is that?
- Viajero: Tráiganos una botella de vino blanco. [TRAY-gah-nohs OO-nah boh-TEH-yah deh BEE-noh BLAHN-koh] = Bring us a bottle of white wine.
- Mesero: ¿Van a querer postre? [BAHN ah keh-REHR POHS-treh] = Will you want dessert?
- Viajera: Sí, deme flan de caramelo. [SEE, DEH-meh FLAHN deh kah-rah-MEH-loh] = Yes, give me caramel custard.
- Viajero: Yo no quiero postre. Solamente tráigame una taza de café y la cuenta, por favor. [yoh noh KYEH-roh POHS-treh. soh-lah-MEHN-teh TRAY-gah-meh OO-nah TAH-sah deh kah-FEH ee lah KWEHN-tah, pohr fah-BOHR] = I don't want dessert. Just bring me a cup of coffee and the bill, please.
  Señores ("gentlemen") is used politely even when a woman is in the group. The course says a 15 to 20 percent tip is customary in more expensive restaurants and 15 percent in moderately priced ones; check a current source before teaching a number.

## Puerto Rican dishes and menu words (Puerto Rico)

## Ordering in a bar or restaurant (Paraguay)
Country: PY (Paraguay)

Source: Peace Corps, "Spanish I and Work Book (For Peace Corps Volunteers)", Unidad Cuatro, "Invitación para cenar" and vocabulary (pp. 48-50), https://www.livelingua.com/course/peace-corps/spanish-language-lessons---paraguay. English glosses are the book's where it gives one, otherwise ours; pronunciations are ours (Latin American: z/ce/ci = "s", ll/y = "y").

- Mozo, ¿qué tiene hoy? [MOH-soh, keh TYEH-neh oy] = Waiter, what do you have today?
- (waiter) Bueno, tenemos milanesa, pollo con ensalada y minutas. [BWEH-noh, teh-NEH-mohs mee-lah-NEH-sah, POH-yoh kohn ehn-sah-LAH-dah ee mee-NOO-tahs] = Well, we have milanesa, chicken with salad and quick dishes.
- ¿Qué vas a pedir vos? [keh bahs ah peh-DEER bohs] = What are you going to order? (to a friend)
- Me gustaría comer milanesa con ensalada mixta. [meh goos-tah-REE-ah koh-MEHR mee-lah-NEH-sah kohn ehn-sah-LAH-dah MEEKS-tah] = I'd like to have milanesa with mixed salad.
- Yo quiero pollo al horno con puré de papas. [yoh KYEH-roh POH-yoh ahl OR-noh kohn poo-REH deh PAH-pahs] = I want roast chicken with mashed potatoes.
- (waiter) ¿Desean tomar algo? [deh-SEH-ahn toh-MAR AHL-goh] = Would you like something to drink?
- Para mí, una gaseosa natural. [PAH-rah MEE, OO-nah gah-seh-OH-sah nah-too-RAHL] = For me, a soft drink at room temperature.
- Entonces, tráiganos una gaseosa natural y una cerveza bien helada. [ehn-TOHN-sehs, TRAY-gah-nohs OO-nah gah-seh-OH-sah nah-too-RAHL ee OO-nah sehr-BEH-sah byehn eh-LAH-dah] = Then bring us a room-temperature soft drink and a very cold beer.
- Nada más, gracias. [NAH-dah mahs, GRAH-syahs] = Nothing else, thanks.
- ¿Querés postre? [keh-REHS POHS-treh] = Do you want dessert? (vos)
- Me gustó muchísimo la cena. [meh goos-TOH moo-CHEE-see-moh lah SEH-nah] = I really enjoyed dinner.
- Por favor, tráiganos la cuenta. [pohr fah-BOR, TRAY-gah-nohs lah KWEHN-tah] = Please bring us the bill.
- Aquí tiene, y el vuelto es su propina. [ah-KEE TYEH-neh, ee ehl BWEHL-toh ehs soo proh-PEE-nah] = Here you are, and the change is your tip.
- el mozo [ehl MOH-soh] = the waiter
- el vuelto [ehl BWEHL-toh] = the change
- helado [eh-LAH-doh] = cold (drinks)
- natural [nah-too-RAHL] = room temperature (drinks)
- fresco [FREHS-koh] = cool
