# Spanish for shopping and money

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Spain" (1984), Module III "Shopping" (seafood market, open-air market, department store) and the "Cultural Notes" (shopping, currency), https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-spanish-headstart-for-spain-course/. US Government work, public domain. Also: Wikivoyage, "Spanish phrasebook" (Shopping, Money), https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Spanish_phrasebook. CC BY-SA 4.0. "¿Tiene cambio? / No, no tengo." from Peace Corps Uruguay, "Español I: Manual" (1993), https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/spanish/courses/peace-corps-spanish-language-course/, public domain. The FSI prices were in pesetas and are left out. Pronunciations follow Spain (z, ce, ci = "th").

## Asking the price
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Cuánto cuesta? [KWAHN-toh KWEHS-tah] = How much does it cost?
- ¿Cuánto vale ese cántaro? [KWAHN-toh BAH-leh EH-seh KAHN-tah-roh] = How much does that pitcher cost?
- ¿Cuánto valen estos sombreros? [KWAHN-toh BAH-lehn EHS-tohs sohm-BREH-rohs] = How much do these hats cost?
- ¿Cuánto quiere por eso? [KWAHN-toh KYEH-reh pohr EH-soh] = How much do you want for that?
- ¿Cuánto quiere por esto? [KWAHN-toh KYEH-reh pohr EHS-toh] = How much do you want for this?
- ¿Qué precio tiene? [keh PREH-thyoh TYEH-neh] = What is the price?
- ¿A cómo está la merluza? [ah KOH-moh ehs-TAH lah mehr-LOO-thah] = How much is the hake? (market phrase for price per kilo)
- ¿A cómo las gambas? [ah KOH-moh lahs GAHM-bahs] = How much are the prawns?
- (seller) A ... euros el kilo. [ah ... EW-rohs ehl KEE-loh] = ... euros a kilo.
- ¿Y ésa? [ee EH-sah] = And that one?

## Too expensive and bargaining
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Por qué tan caro? [pohr KEH tahn KAH-roh] = Why so expensive?
- Es demasiado caro. [ehs deh-mah-SYAH-doh KAH-roh] = That's too expensive.
- Es muy caro para mí. [ehs MOO-ee KAH-roh PAH-rah MEE] = I can't afford it.
- ¿No me hace una rebajita? [noh meh AH-theh OO-nah rreh-bah-HEE-tah] = Won't you give me a small discount?
- Me lo deja en ..., ¿no? [meh loh DEH-hah ehn ..., noh] = You'll let me have it for ..., won't you?
- (seller) No puede ser, señora. [noh PWEH-deh sehr, seh-NYOH-rah] = It can't be done, madam.
- (seller) Está hecha a mano. [ehs-TAH EH-chah ah MAH-noh] = It's handmade.
- (seller) Bueno, lléveselo por ... [BWEH-noh, YEH-beh-seh-loh pohr] = OK, take it for ...
- No me interesa. [noh meh een-teh-REH-sah] = I'm not interested.
- caro = expensive; barato = cheap
  You may bargain in open-air street markets (mercados al aire libre), never in shops. Department stores have fixed prices.

## Buying it
Country: ES (Spain)

- Me llevo los pendientes. [meh YEH-boh lohs pehn-DYEHN-tehs] = I'll take the earrings.
- Entonces me llevo la mantelería y el cántaro. [ehn-TOHN-thehs meh YEH-boh lah mahn-teh-leh-REE-ah ee ehl KAHN-tah-roh] = Then I'll take the tablecloth set and the pitcher.
- De acuerdo, me lo llevaré. [deh ah-KWEHR-doh, meh loh yeh-bah-REH] = OK, I'll take it.
- ¿Tiene una bolsa? [TYEH-neh OO-nah BOHL-sah] = Can I have a bag?
- ¿De qué es? [deh KEH ehs] = What is it made of?
- (seller) Es de plata. [ehs deh PLAH-tah] = It's silver.
- de cuero = leather; de plata = silver; de oro = gold; de madera = wood; de lino = linen; de plástico = plastic

## Clothes and sizes
Country: ES (Spain)

- (clerk) ¿Qué desea? [keh deh-SEH-ah] = What would you like? / Can I help you?
- Quisiera una camisa de verano. [kee-SYEH-rah OO-nah kah-MEE-sah deh beh-RAH-noh] = I would like a summer shirt.
- (clerk) ¿Qué talla usa? [keh TAH-yah OO-sah] = What size do you wear?
- No sé. Las tallas americanas son diferentes. [noh SEH. lahs TAH-yahs ah-meh-ree-KAH-nahs sohn dee-feh-REHN-tehs] = I don't know. American sizes are different.
- ¿Tiene esto de mi talla? [TYEH-neh EHS-toh deh mee TAH-yah] = Do you have this in my size?
- Me gusta ésa azul. ¿Puedo probármela? [meh GOOS-tah EH-sah ah-THOOL. PWEH-doh proh-BAR-meh-lah] = I like that blue one. May I try it on?
- ¿Puedo probármelos? [PWEH-doh proh-BAR-meh-lohs] = May I try them on? (for zapatos, shoes)
## Trying clothes on
Country: ES (Spain)

- No me queda bien, ¿verdad? [noh meh KEH-dah byehn, behr-DAHD] = It doesn't fit me, does it?
- (clerk) Es demasiado pequeña para usted. Pruébese ésta. [ehs deh-mah-SYAH-doh peh-KEH-nyah PAH-rah oos-TEHD. PRWEH-beh-seh EHS-tah] = It's too small for you. Try this one on.
- Esta me queda mejor. [EHS-tah meh KEH-dah meh-HOR] = This one fits me better.
- Quisiera unos zapatos como éstos, pero del número nueve. [kee-SYEH-rah OO-nohs thah-PAH-tohs KOH-moh EHS-tohs, PEH-roh dehl NOO-meh-roh NWEH-beh] = I'd like shoes like these, but size nine. (Shoe sizes use del número, clothes use talla.)

## Paying
Country: ES (Spain)

- (clerk) Sírvase pagar en la caja. [SEER-bah-seh pah-GAR ehn lah KAH-hah] = Kindly pay at the cash register.
- (clerk) Pase a la caja, por favor. [PAH-seh ah lah KAH-hah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Please go to the cash register.
- ¿Aceptan tarjeta de crédito? [ah-THEHP-tahn tar-HEH-tah deh KREH-dee-toh] = Do you accept credit cards?
- ¿Aceptan dólares estadounidenses? [ah-THEHP-tahn DOH-lah-rehs ehs-tah-dow-oo-nee-DEHN-sehs] = Do you accept US dollars?
- (clerk) ¿Tiene cambio? [TYEH-neh KAHM-byoh] = Do you have change?
- No, no tengo. [noh, noh TEHN-goh] = No, I don't.
- Aquí tiene. [ah-KEE TYEH-neh] = Here you are.
- Necesito monedas. [neh-theh-SEE-toh moh-NEH-dahs] = I need coins.

## Money and the euro
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Dónde hay un cajero automático? [DOHN-deh AY oon kah-HEH-roh ow-toh-MAH-tee-koh] = Where is a cash machine (ATM)?
- ¿Dónde puedo cambiar dinero? [DOHN-deh PWEH-doh kahm-BYAR dee-NEH-roh] = Where can I change money?
- ¿A cuánto está el cambio? [ah KWAHN-toh ehs-TAH ehl KAHM-byoh] = What is the exchange rate?
- el dinero = money; la tarjeta de crédito = credit card; el billete = banknote; la moneda = coin; el cambio = change / exchange; la caja = cash register
  Spain's currency is the euro (since 1 January 2002; before that, the peseta). Banks give the best exchange rates; exchange offices (cambios) are open long hours but give worse rates. Always check the current rate.

## Markets and food shopping
Country: ES (Spain)

- (seller) ¿Qué le pongo hoy? [keh leh POHN-goh OY] = What can I get you today?
- Póngame dos kilos de patatas, un kilo de tomates, y dos lechugas. [POHN-gah-meh dohs KEE-lohs deh pah-TAH-tahs, oon KEE-loh deh toh-MAH-tehs, ee dohs leh-CHOO-gahs] = Give me two kilos of potatoes, a kilo of tomatoes and two lettuces.
- Deme medio kilo. [DEH-meh MEH-dyoh KEE-loh] = Give me half a kilo.
- Deme un cuarto de kilo. [DEH-meh oon KWAR-toh deh KEE-loh] = Give me a quarter of a kilo.
- (seller) ¿Algo más? [AHL-goh mahs] = Anything else?
- Nada más. [NAH-dah mahs] = That's all.
- Nada más por hoy. [NAH-dah mahs pohr OY] = That's all for today.
- patatas = potatoes (papas in Latin America); tomates = tomatoes; cebollas = onions; naranjas = oranges; manzanas = apples; plátanos = bananas; uva = grapes; pescado = fish; carne = meat; pollo = chicken; huevos = eggs
  Traditionally (FSI notes, 1984) shops open about 8:30 to 1:30, close for lunch, and reopen until 8 or 9 pm. Department stores keep regular hours. Check current opening times locally. They are usually closed on Sundays and holidays. El Corte Inglés is a big department store chain. El Rastro is Madrid's Sunday-morning flea market.

## Shop names ending in -ería (Libro Libre)

- Many shops end in -ería, like "-ery" in English (bakery).
- la panadería = bakery;  la carnicería = butcher's;  la frutería = fruit shop
- la librería = bookshop (not library: that is la biblioteca);  la papelería = stationery shop
- la zapatería = shoe shop;  la heladería = ice-cream shop;  la floristería = florist
- la juguetería = toy shop

## Buying food at the market (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module III Unit 1 "Buying Seafood and Other Groceries" (pp. 128-130) and Module III Glossary (pp. 174-182); set in Panama, https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours. The 1980s prices in the dialogue are left out.

- ¿A cómo está la libra de pargo? [ah KOH-moh ehs-TAH lah LEE-brah deh PAHR-goh] = How much is a pound of red snapper?
- Deme libra y media. [DEH-meh LEE-brah ee MEH-dyah] = Give me a pound and a half.
- (seller) Aquí tiene. ¿Qué otra cosita? [ah-KEE TYEH-neh. keh OH-trah koh-SEE-tah] = Here you are. Anything else?
- Camarones. ¿A cómo están? [kah-mah-ROH-nehs. ah KOH-moh ehs-TAHN] = Shrimp. How much are they?
- Deme dos libras, por favor. [DEH-meh dohs LEE-brahs, pohr fah-BOHR] = Give me two pounds, please.
- (seller) ¿Qué desea, señora? [keh deh-SEH-ah, seh-NYOH-rah] = What would you like, ma'am?
- Deme dos lechugas, una libra de tomates y cuatro libras de yuca. [DEH-meh dohs leh-CHOO-gahs, OO-nah LEE-brah deh toh-MAH-tehs ee KWAH-troh LEE-brahs deh YOO-kah] = Give me two heads of lettuce, a pound of tomatoes and four pounds of yucca.
- (seller) Sí, cómo no. Y fruta, ¿no va a comprar hoy? [see, KOH-moh noh. ee FROO-tah, noh bah ah kohm-PRAHR oy] = Sure. And fruit, aren't you going to buy some today?
- Sí, deme ocho mangos y esa piña grande, por favor. [see, DEH-meh OH-choh MAHN-gohs ee EH-sah PEE-nyah GRAHN-deh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Yes, give me eight mangos and that big pineapple, please.
- ¿Cuánto es por todo? [KWAHN-toh ehs pohr TOH-doh] = How much is it for everything?
  In this Panama course, food is sold by the pound (la libra), not by the kilo.

## Bargaining for souvenirs (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module III Unit 2 "Buying Souvenirs" (pp. 144-146) and Module III Glossary (pp. 174-182); set in Panama City, https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- ¿Cuánto quiere por esa mola? [KWAHN-toh KYEH-reh pohr EH-sah MOH-lah] = How much do you want for that mola? (Don't use ¿Cuánto quiere? where prices are fixed.)
- ¿Por qué tan cara? [pohr KEH tahn KAH-rah] = Why so expensive?
- (seller) Porque es muy fina. [POHR-keh ehs mooy FEE-nah] = Because it's of very good quality.
- ¿No me la deja en veinte dólares? [noh meh lah DEH-hah ehn BAYN-teh DOH-lah-rehs] = Won't you let me have it for twenty dollars?
- (seller) En veinte no, pero se la dejo en veintidós. [ehn BAYN-teh noh, PEH-roh seh lah DEH-hoh ehn bayn-tee-DOHS] = Not for twenty, but I'll let you have it for twenty-two.
- Y ese cenicero, ¿de qué está hecho? [ee EH-seh seh-nee-SEH-roh, deh keh ehs-TAH EH-choh] = And that ashtray, what is it made of?
- (seller) De piedra de jabón, señor. [deh PYEH-drah deh hah-BOHN, seh-NYOHR] = Out of soapstone, sir.
- (seller) Bueno, se lo dejo en dos. [BWEH-noh, seh loh DEH-hoh ehn dohs] = Well, I'll let you have it for two.
- hecho a mano [EH-choh ah MAH-noh] = handmade
  A mola is a panel of layered, cut-out cotton cloth made by the Cuna (Guna) people of Panama's San Blas Islands. Molas are sold in the streets and plazas of Panama City.

## Buying clothes (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module III Unit 3 "At a Department Store" (pp. 158-160) and Module III Glossary (pp. 174-182); set in Panama, https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours. The 1980s price in the dialogue is left out.

- (clerk) ¿Qué desea, caballero? [keh deh-SEH-ah, kah-bah-YEH-roh] = What would you like, sir?
- Busco una guayabera. [BOOS-koh OO-nah gwah-yah-BEH-rah] = I'm looking for a guayabera. (an embroidered shirt worn outside the trousers)
- (clerk) ¿Qué tamaño usa? [keh tah-MAH-nyoh OO-sah] = What size do you wear?
- Me gusta ésta de color azul. [meh GOOS-tah EHS-tah deh koh-LOHR ah-SOOL] = I like this blue one.
- ¿Puedo probármela? [PWEH-doh proh-BAHR-meh-lah] = Can I try it on? (probármelo for a masculine word)
- (clerk) Por supuesto, señor. [pohr soo-PWEHS-toh, seh-NYOHR] = Of course, sir.
- No me queda bien. Es muy pequeña. [noh meh KEH-dah byehn. ehs mooy peh-KEH-nyah] = It doesn't fit me. It's too small.
- ¿Puedo pagar con VISA? [PWEH-doh pah-GAHR kohn BEE-sah] = Can I pay with VISA?
- (clerk) Pague en la caja, por favor. [PAH-geh ehn lah KAH-hah, pohr fah-BOHR] = Pay at the register, please.
- una camisa de manga corta [OO-nah kah-MEE-sah deh MAHN-gah KOHR-tah] = a short-sleeved shirt
- una camisa de manga larga [OO-nah kah-MEE-sah deh MAHN-gah LAHR-gah] = a long-sleeved shirt
- un par de zapatos [oon pahr deh sah-PAH-tohs] = a pair of shoes
  In Panama, English sizes (small, medium, large) are used for some clothes. The course notes (1980s) warned that paying by credit card could cost 5 to 10 percent more.

## At the market (Puerto Rico)
Country: PR (Puerto Rico)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Puerto Rico", Module III Unit 1 "Buying Seafood and Groceries" (conversation p. 121, exercises pp. 122-129) and Module III Glossary (pp. 164-167), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-puerto-rico. - Vendedor: Aquí tiene. ¿Quiere algo más? [ah-KEE TYEH-neh. KYEH-reh AHL-goh MAHS] = Here you are. Do you want anything else?

- Vendedor: A la orden. [ah lah OR-dehn] = You're welcome. / At your service.
- la libra [lah LEE-brah] = the pound (food is sold by the pound)
- la china [lah CHEE-nah] = orange (the fruit)
- el guineo [ehl gee-NEH-oh] = banana
- el plátano [ehl PLAH-tah-noh] = plantain (a large banana that is cooked)
- la yautía [lah yow-TEE-ah] = yautia (a root vegetable)
- el aguacate [ehl ah-gwah-KAH-teh] = avocado
- el mangó [ehl mahn-GOH] = mango
- la guayaba [lah gwah-YAH-bah] = guava
- el pargo [ehl PAR-goh] = red snapper
- el mero [ehl MEH-roh] = grouper
- la carne molida [lah KAR-neh moh-LEE-dah] = ground beef
- la carne de guisar [lah KAR-neh deh gee-SAR] = stew meat

## Souvenirs and clothes (Puerto Rico)
Country: PR (Puerto Rico)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Puerto Rico", Module III Unit 2 "Buying Souvenirs" (conversation and notes pp. 137-139) and Unit 3 "At a Clothing Store" (conversation and notes pp. 151-153), Module III Glossary (pp. 164-168), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-puerto-rico. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- ¿Cuánto cuesta esa hamaca? [KWAHN-toh KWEHS-tah EH-sah ah-MAH-kah] = How much does that hammock cost?
- ¿Por qué tan cara? [pohr KEH tahn KAH-rah] = Why so expensive?
- (seller) Porque es hecha a mano y es muy fina. [POHR-keh ehs EH-chah ah MAH-noh ee ehs MOO-ee FEE-nah] = Because it's handmade and very fine.
- Y ese cenicero, ¿de qué está hecho? [ee EH-seh seh-nee-SEH-roh, deh KEH ehs-TAH EH-choh] = And that ashtray, what is it made of?
- (seller) De plata, señor. [deh PLAH-tah, seh-NYOR] = Silver, sir.
- la canasta [lah kah-NAHS-tah] = the basket
- el caracol [ehl kah-rah-KOHL] = the conch shell
- (seller) ¿Qué desea, caballero? [KEH deh-SEH-ah, kah-bah-YEH-roh] = What would you like, sir?
- Busco una guayabera. [BOOS-koh OO-nah gwah-yah-BEH-rah] = I'm looking for a guayabera.
- (seller) ¿Qué tamaño usa? [KEH tah-MAH-nyoh OO-sah] = What size do you wear?
- No me queda bien. Es muy pequeña. [noh meh KEH-dah BYEHN. ehs MOO-ee peh-KEH-nyah] = It doesn't fit me. It's very small.
- Me queda bien. [meh KEH-dah BYEHN] = It fits me.

- las chancletas [lahs chahn-KLEH-tahs] = slippers

  Souvenir shops sell traditional handcrafts: santos (small religious figures carved of wood), ceramics, lace, baskets, hammocks, jewelry, maracas and guitars.

## Buying food: shops and snack bars (Paraguay)
Country: PY (Paraguay)

Source: Peace Corps, "Spanish I and Work Book (For Peace Corps Volunteers)", Unidad Cuatro, "De compras", "En la cantina" and vocabulary (pp. 46, 48-50), https://www.livelingua.com/course/peace-corps/spanish-language-lessons---paraguay. Peace Corps (US Government) training material; the ERIC copy states it is federally funded or otherwise in the public domain. English glosses are the book's where it gives one, otherwise ours; pronunciations are ours (Latin American: z/ce/ci = "s", ll/y = "y").
