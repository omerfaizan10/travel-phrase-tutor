# Everyday Spanish conversation: questions, small talk, goodbyes and emergencies

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Spain" (1984), Module I Units 2-3 (small talk, work, family, leaving) and keys, https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-spanish-headstart-for-spain-course/. ## Question words
Country: ALL (any Spanish-speaking country)

- ¿Qué? [keh] = What?
- ¿Quién? [kyehn] = Who?
- ¿Cómo? [KOH-moh] = How?
- ¿Dónde? [DOHN-deh] = Where?
- ¿De dónde? [deh DOHN-deh] = Where from?
- ¿Adónde? [ah-DOHN-deh] = Where to?
- ¿Cuándo? [KWAHN-doh] = When?
- ¿A qué hora? [ah keh OH-rah] = At what time?
- ¿Cuánto? [KWAHN-toh] = How much?
- ¿Cuántos? / ¿Cuántas? [KWAHN-tohs / KWAHN-tahs] = How many?
- ¿Cuál? [kwahl] = Which? / What?
- ¿Por qué? [pohr KEH] = Why?
- ¿De qué? [deh KEH] = Of what? / What (is it made) of?
- ¿Verdad? / ¿No? [behr-DAHD / noh] = Right? / Isn't it? (added to the end of a sentence)

## Small talk
Country: ES (Spain)

- Ustedes son americanos, ¿verdad? [oos-TEH-dehs sohn ah-meh-ree-KAH-nohs, behr-DAHD] = You're Americans, aren't you?
- ¿Hace mucho que estás aquí? [AH-theh MOO-choh keh ehs-TAHS ah-KEE] = Have you been here long? (informal)
- Hace dos semanas que estoy en España. [AH-theh dohs seh-MAH-nahs keh ehs-TOY ehn ehs-PAH-nyah] = I have been in Spain for two weeks.
- ¡Qué bien habláis el español! [keh byehn ah-BLAH-ees ehl ehs-pah-NYOHL] = How well you (all) speak Spanish! (a compliment you may hear; habláis is the Spain-only vosotros form)
- ¡Oh, no! Tenemos que aprender mucho más. [oh, noh. teh-NEH-mohs keh ah-prehn-DEHR MOO-choh mahs] = Not really. We have to learn much more.
- ¿Te gusta España? [teh GOOS-tah ehs-PAH-nyah] = Do you like Spain?
- Me encanta Madrid, especialmente por la noche. [meh ehn-KAHN-tah mah-DREETH, ehs-peh-thyahl-MEHN-teh pohr lah NOH-cheh] = I love Madrid, especially at night.
## Small talk: work and family
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿En qué trabaja usted? [ehn keh trah-BAH-hah oos-TEHD] = What do you do (for work)? (formal)
- ¿En qué trabajas? [ehn keh trah-BAH-hahs] = What do you do? (informal)
- ¿Dónde trabaja usted? [DOHN-deh trah-BAH-hah oos-TEHD] = Where do you work?
- Soy maestra, pero no trabajo ahora. [soy mah-EHS-trah, PEH-roh noh trah-BAH-hoh ah-OH-rah] = I'm a teacher, but I'm not working now.
- ¿Tenéis niños? [teh-NEH-ees NEE-nyohs] = Do you (two) have children? (vosotros form, used with friends in Spain)
- Sí, tenemos dos. [SEE, teh-NEH-mohs dohs] = Yes, we have two.
- No tenemos niños. [noh teh-NEH-mohs NEE-nyohs] = We don't have children.
## Small talk: short replies
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Qué hay? [keh AY] = How's it going? (casual)
- (reply) Ahí, tirando. / Vamos tirando. [ah-EE, tee-RAHN-doh / BAH-mohs tee-RAHN-doh] = Getting along.
- ¿Por qué no nos tuteamos? [pohr KEH noh nohs too-teh-AH-mohs] = Why don't we say tú to each other?
- Desde luego. [DEHS-deh LWEH-goh] = Of course. / Sure.
- Claro. [KLAH-roh] = Sure.
- Por supuesto. [pohr soo-PWEHS-toh] = Of course.
- Más o menos. [mahs oh MEH-nohs] = More or less.

## Saying goodbye
Country: ES (Spain)

- Adiós. [ah-DYOHS] = Goodbye.
- Hasta luego. [AHS-tah LWEH-goh] = See you later. / Goodbye.
- Hasta mañana. [AHS-tah mah-NYAH-nah] = See you tomorrow.
- Hasta el viernes. [AHS-tah ehl BYEHR-nehs] = See you Friday.
- Buenas noches. [BWEH-nahs NOH-chehs] = Good night.
- Lo siento, pero tenemos que irnos. [loh SYEHN-toh, PEH-roh teh-NEH-mohs keh EER-nohs] = I'm sorry, but we have to go.
- Tengo que irme. [TEHN-goh keh EER-meh] = I have to go.
- ¿No te parece que ya es hora de irnos? [noh teh pah-REH-theh keh yah ehs OH-rah deh EER-nohs] = Don't you think it's time to go?
- Encantado de hablar contigo. [ehn-kahn-TAH-doh deh ah-BLAR kohn-TEE-goh] = Nice talking to you. (to one friend; a woman says encantada)
- Encantado de hablar con vosotros. [ehn-kahn-TAH-doh deh ah-BLAR kohn boh-SOH-trohs] = Nice talking to you. (to several friends; vosotros is used in Spain, ustedes in Latin America)
- A ver si nos vemos pronto. [ah behr see nohs BEH-mohs PROHN-toh] = Hope to see you soon.
- Que pase un buen día. [keh PAH-seh oon bwehn DEE-ah] = Have a good day. (formal)

## Emergencies
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¡Socorro! / ¡Ayuda! [soh-KOH-rroh / ah-YOO-dah] = Help!
- ¡Cuidado! [kwee-DAH-doh] = Look out!
- ¡Fuego! [FWEH-goh] = Fire!
- ¡Policía! [poh-lee-THEE-ah] = Police!
- ¡Llame a la policía! [YAH-meh ah lah poh-lee-THEE-ah] = Call the police!
- ¡Ladrón! [lah-DROHN] = Thief!
- Es una emergencia. [ehs OO-nah eh-mehr-HEHN-thyah] = It's an emergency.
- Necesito ayuda. [neh-theh-SEE-toh ah-YOO-dah] = I need help.
- ¿Puede usted ayudarme, por favor? [PWEH-deh oos-TEHD ah-yoo-DAR-meh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Can you help me, please?
- ¡Hubo un accidente! [OO-boh oon ahk-thee-DEHN-teh] = There's been an accident!
- ¡Llame a una ambulancia! [YAH-meh ah OO-nah ahm-boo-LAHN-thyah] = Call an ambulance!
- ¡Llame a un doctor! [YAH-meh ah oon dohk-TOR] = Call a doctor!
- ¿Dónde está la comisaría? [DOHN-deh ehs-TAH lah koh-mee-sah-REE-ah] = Where is the police station?
- ¿Puedo usar su teléfono? [PWEH-doh oo-SAR soo teh-LEH-foh-noh] = Can I use your phone?
- ¿Me presta su móvil? [meh PREHS-tah soo MOH-beel] = Can I borrow your mobile phone? (móvil in Spain, celular in Latin America)
- Necesito llamar a la embajada. [neh-theh-SEE-toh yah-MAR ah lah ehm-bah-HAH-dah] = I need to call the embassy.
  The emergency number in Spain (and the whole European Union) is 112. It is free. Call 112 if you are the victim of a crime.

## Problems: lost, robbed, misunderstanding
Country: ES (Spain)

- Estoy perdido. (m.) / Estoy perdida. (f.) [ehs-TOY pehr-DEE-doh / pehr-DEE-dah] = I'm lost.
- Perdí la cartera. [pehr-DEE lah kar-TEH-rah] = I lost my wallet.
- Perdí mi bolso. [pehr-DEE mee BOHL-soh] = I lost my handbag.
- Déjame en paz. [DEH-hah-meh ehn pahth] = Leave me alone.
- Fue un malentendido. [fweh oon mah-lehn-tehn-DEE-doh] = It was a misunderstanding.
- Quiero hablar con la embajada estadounidense. [KYEH-roh ah-BLAR kohn lah ehm-bah-HAH-dah ehs-tah-dow-oo-nee-DEHN-seh] = I want to speak to the American embassy.
- una denuncia = a police report (ask for a copy for insurance or a new passport)

## Feeling ill
Country: ES (Spain)

- Estoy enfermo. (m.) / Estoy enferma. (f.) [ehs-TOY ehn-FEHR-moh / ehn-FEHR-mah] = I'm ill.
- Me siento mal. [meh SYEHN-toh mahl] = I feel ill.
- Necesito un médico. [neh-theh-SEE-toh oon MEH-dee-koh] = I need a doctor.
- (doctor) ¿Qué le pasa? [keh leh PAH-sah] = What's the matter?
- Me duele todo el cuerpo, tengo dolor de cabeza. [meh DWEH-leh TOH-doh ehl KWEHR-poh, TEHN-goh doh-LOR deh kah-BEH-thah] = My whole body hurts, I have a headache.
- tener fiebre = to have a fever; tener tos = to have a cough; tener dolor de estómago = to have a stomach ache; tener dolor de garganta = to have a sore throat; tener gripe = to have flu
- (doctor) No se preocupe. [noh seh preh-oh-KOO-peh] = Don't worry.
- Necesito analgésico. [neh-theh-SEE-toh ah-nahl-HEH-see-koh] = I need a painkiller.
- Estoy mucho mejor. [ehs-TOY MOO-choh meh-HOR] = I'm much better.

## Small talk: living here and family (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module I Unit 2 "How Long Have You Been Living in Panama?" and Unit 3 "Where Do You Live?" (pp. 22-24, 36-38; set in Panama), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- ¿Cuánto tiempo hace que vive usted en Panamá? [KWAHN-toh TYEHM-poh AH-seh keh BEE-beh oos-TEHD ehn pah-nah-MAH] = How long have you been living in Panama?
- Hace dos meses que vivo aquí. [AH-seh dohs MEH-sehs keh BEE-boh ah-KEE] = I've been living here for two months.
- ¿Le gusta Panamá? [leh GOOS-tah pah-nah-MAH] = Do you like Panama?
- Sí, me gusta, pero hace mucho calor. [see, meh GOOS-tah, PEH-roh AH-seh MOO-choh kah-LOHR] = Yes, I like it, but it's very hot.
- ¿Tienen ustedes hijos? [TYEH-nehn oos-TEH-dehs EE-hohs] = Do you have children? (to a couple; hijos = children or sons, hijas = daughters)
- Sí, tenemos dos hijos, un niño y una niña. [see, teh-NEH-mohs dohs EE-hohs, oon NEE-nyoh ee OO-nah NEE-nyah] = Yes, we have two children, a boy and a girl.
- ¿Dónde viven ustedes? [DOHN-deh BEE-behn oos-TEH-dehs] = Where do you live? (to more than one person)
- Lo siento, amigos, pero tengo que irme. [loh SYEHN-toh, ah-MEE-gohs, PEH-roh TEHN-goh keh EER-meh] = I'm sorry, friends, but I have to leave.
- Hasta la vista. [AHS-tah lah BEES-tah] = See you later.

## At the doctor, with vos (Paraguay)
Country: PY (Paraguay)

Source: Peace Corps, "Spanish I and Work Book (For Peace Corps Volunteers)", Unidad Cinco, "¿Qué te pasa?" and vocabulary (pp. 56-57), https://www.livelingua.com/course/peace-corps/spanish-language-lessons---paraguay. Peace Corps (US Government) training material; the ERIC copy states it is federally funded or otherwise in the public domain. English glosses are the book's where it gives one, otherwise ours; pronunciations are ours (Latin American: z/ce/ci = "s", ll/y = "y").

- (doctor) Hola, ¿qué te pasa? [OH-lah, keh teh PAH-sah] = Hi, what's the matter? (informal)
- (doctor) ¿Te duele la espalda? [teh DWEH-leh lah ehs-PAHL-dah] = Does your back hurt?
- (doctor) Vamos a ver, respirá hondo. [BAH-mohs ah behr, rrehs-pee-RAH OHN-doh] = Let's see, breathe deeply. (vos command)
- (doctor) No tenés nada grave, sólo una gripe muy fuerte. [noh teh-NEHS NAH-dah GRAH-beh, SOH-loh OO-nah GREE-peh MOO-ee FWEHR-teh] = It's nothing serious, just a very bad flu.
- (doctor) Te voy a recetar unas pastillas para tomar cada cuatro horas. [teh boy ah rreh-seh-TAR OO-nahs pahs-TEE-yahs PAH-rah toh-MAR KAH-dah KWAH-troh OH-rahs] = I'm going to prescribe some pills for you to take every four hours.
- (doctor) Si la fiebre continúa, vení por aquí otra vez. [see lah FYEH-breh kohn-tee-NOO-ah, beh-NEE pohr ah-KEE OH-trah behs] = If the fever continues, come back here again.
- Bueno, me siento muy mal. [BWEH-noh, meh SYEHN-toh MOO-ee mahl] = OK, I feel very ill.
- ¿Cómo te sentís hoy? [KOH-moh teh sehn-TEES oy] = How do you feel today? (vos)
- ¿Qué te pasa a vos? [keh teh PAH-sah ah bohs] = What's the matter with you? (vos)
- Estoy peor. [ehs-TOY peh-OR] = I'm worse.
- tener diarrea [teh-NEHR dyah-RREH-ah] = to have diarrhea
- tener dolor de oído [teh-NEHR doh-LOR deh oh-EE-doh] = to have an earache

## At the doctor: questions you may hear (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Language Training Facility, U.S.A. John F. Kennedy Center for Special Warfare (Airborne), Fort Bragg, "Spanish Useful Medical Phrases" (February 1978), pp. 1-3, 5, 16, 18, 20, 22, https://www.livelingua.com/course/dli/spanish-language-course---modular-basic-course (listed by Live Lingua under DLI, Modular Basic Course). US Army training material. English glosses are the booklet's unless marked; pronunciations are ours (Latin American: z/ce/ci = "s"). The booklet names no country. It was written for US military medical staff, so the doctor uses usted.

- Hable despacio. [AH-bleh dehs-PAH-syoh] = Speak slowly.
- ¿Me entiende usted? [meh ehn-TYEHN-deh oos-TEHD] = Do you understand me?
- Escríbalo aquí. [ehs-KREE-bah-loh ah-KEE] = Write it here.
- (doctor) ¿Qué le pasa a usted? [keh leh PAH-sah ah oos-TEHD] = What's the matter with you?
- (doctor) ¿Tiene fiebre? [TYEH-neh FYEH-breh] = Do you have a fever?
- (doctor) ¿Tiene escalofríos? [TYEH-neh ehs-kah-loh-FREE-ohs] = Do you have chills?
- (doctor) ¿Tiene dolor en alguna parte? [TYEH-neh doh-LOR ehn ahl-GOO-nah PAR-teh] = Do you have pain anywhere?
- (doctor) ¿Le duele algo? [leh DWEH-leh AHL-goh] = Does anything hurt? (gloss ours)
- (doctor) ¿Dónde le duele? [DOHN-deh leh DWEH-leh] = Where does it hurt?
- (doctor) ¿Desde cuándo? [DEHS-deh KWAHN-doh] = Since when?
- (doctor) ¿Cuánto tiempo ha estado enfermo? (to a man) / ¿Cuánto tiempo ha estado enferma? (to a woman) [KWAHN-toh TYEHM-poh ah ehs-TAH-doh ehn-FEHR-moh / ehn-FEHR-mah] = How long have you been sick?
- (doctor) ¿Le duele aquí? [leh DWEH-leh ah-KEE] = Any pain here?
- (doctor) ¿Le duele al respirar? [leh DWEH-leh ahl rrehs-pee-RAR] = Does it hurt you to breathe?
- (doctor) ¿Se siente mareado? [seh SYEHN-teh mah-reh-AH-doh] = Do you feel dizzy? (to a woman: mareada)
- (doctor) ¿Siente que se va a desmayar? [SYEHN-teh keh seh bah ah dehs-mah-YAR] = Do you feel like fainting?
- (doctor) Señale con el dedo dónde siente el dolor. [seh-NYAH-leh kohn ehl DEH-doh DOHN-deh SYEHN-teh ehl doh-LOR] = Point with your finger where you feel the pain.
- (doctor) Siéntese aquí. [SYEHN-teh-seh ah-KEE] = Sit down here.
- (doctor) Abra la boca. [AH-brah lah BOH-kah] = Open your mouth.
- (doctor) Respire hondo. [rrehs-PEE-reh OHN-doh] = Breathe deeply.
- (doctor) ¿Lo han picado los insectos? [loh ahn pee-KAH-doh lohs een-SEHK-tohs] = Have you been bitten by insects?
- (doctor) ¿Lo ha mordido un animal? [loh ah mor-DEE-doh oon ah-nee-MAHL] = Have you been bitten by an animal?
- la alergia [ah-LEHR-hyah] = allergy
- el mosquito; la garrapata; la abeja; el alacrán [mohs-KEE-toh; gah-rrah-PAH-tah; ah-BEH-hah; ah-lah-KRAHN] = mosquito; tick; bee; scorpion
