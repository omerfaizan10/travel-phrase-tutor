# Spanish basics: greetings, polite words, numbers, days and time

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Spain" (1984), Modules I-II and answer keys, https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/fsi-spanish-headstart-for-spain-course/. US Government work, public domain. Greetings, introductions, numbers, days and clock times. Also: Wikivoyage, "Spanish phrasebook", https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Spanish_phrasebook. CC BY-SA 4.0. Polite words, understanding each other, months. Pronunciations follow Spain (castellano): z, and c before e or i, sound like English "th".

## Greetings
Country: ES (Spain)

- Buenos días. [BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs] = Good morning.
- Buenas tardes. [BWEH-nahs TAR-dehs] = Good afternoon / good evening (used from lunch until dusk; lunch in Spain is between one and three).
- Buenas noches. [BWEH-nahs NOH-chehs] = Good evening / good night.
- Hola. [OH-lah] = Hi / hello.
- Buenas. [BWEH-nahs] = Hello (casual, short for buenos días / buenas tardes / buenas noches).
- ¿Cómo está usted? [KOH-moh ehs-TAH oos-TEHD] = How are you? (formal)
- ¿Qué tal? [keh TAHL] = How are you? (less formal)
- Muy bien, gracias. [MOO-ee BYEHN, GRAH-thyahs] = Very well, thank you.
- Bien, gracias. ¿Y usted? [BYEHN, GRAH-thyahs. ee oos-TEHD] = Fine, thank you. And you?
- Así, así. [ah-SEE ah-SEE] = So-so.

## Polite words
Country: ES (Spain)

- Por favor. [pohr fah-BOHR] = Please.
- Gracias. [GRAH-thyahs] = Thank you.
- Muchas gracias. [MOO-chahs GRAH-thyahs] = Thank you very much.
- De nada. [deh NAH-dah] = You're welcome.
- Sí. [SEE] = Yes.
- Disculpe. [dees-KOOL-peh] = Excuse me (to get someone's attention).
- Perdone. [pehr-DOH-neh] = Excuse me / pardon me.
- Perdón. [pehr-DOHN] = Excuse me / sorry.
- Permiso. [pehr-MEE-soh] = Excuse me (may I get by?).
- Lo siento. [loh SYEHN-toh] = I'm sorry.
- Usted es muy amable. [oos-TEHD ehs MOO-ee ah-MAH-bleh] = You are very kind.
- Vale. [BAH-leh] = OK. (Used in Spain very much like "OK". As a question, ¿vale? means "Is that OK with you?")

## Introductions
Country: ES (Spain)

- Mucho gusto. [MOO-choh GOOS-toh] = Pleased to meet you.
- Encantado. (m.) / Encantada. (f.) [ehn-kahn-TAH-doh / ehn-kahn-TAH-dah] = Delighted (to meet you). A man says encantado, a woman says encantada.
- Me llamo ... [meh YAH-moh] = My name is ...
- ¿Cómo se llama usted? [KOH-moh seh YAH-mah oos-TEHD] = What is your name? (formal)
- ¿Cómo te llamas? [KOH-moh teh YAH-mahs] = What is your name? (informal)
- Esta es mi mujer. [EHS-tah ehs mee moo-HEHR] = This is my wife. (In Spain say mi mujer. Mi esposa is customary in Latin America.)
- Este es mi marido. [EHS-teh ehs mee mah-REE-doh] = This is my husband.
- Este es mi amigo Manolo. [EHS-teh ehs mee ah-MEE-goh mah-NOH-loh] = This is my friend Manolo.
- Quiero presentarles a unos amigos. [KYEH-roh preh-sehn-TAR-lehs ah OO-nohs ah-MEE-gohs] = I'd like to introduce some friends to you.
## Where you are from and what you like
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿De dónde es usted? [deh DOHN-deh ehs oos-TEHD] = Where are you from? (formal)
- ¿De dónde eres? [deh DOHN-deh EH-rehs] = Where are you from? (informal)
- Soy de California. [soy deh kah-lee-FOR-nyah] = I am from California.
- Somos de los Estados Unidos. [SOH-mohs deh lohs ehs-TAH-dohs oo-NEE-dohs] = We are from the United States.
- Tengo ... años. [TEHN-goh ... AH-nyohs] = I am ... years old.
- ¿Te gusta España? [teh GOOS-tah ehs-PAH-nyah] = Do you like Spain? (informal)
- Sí, me gusta mucho. [SEE, meh GOOS-tah MOO-choh] = Yes, I like it a lot.
- Me encanta. [meh ehn-KAHN-tah] = I love it.
  Note: Spaniards often switch from usted to the familiar tú during a conversation. As a foreigner, wait for the Spaniard to start using tú.

## Understanding each other
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Habla usted inglés? [AH-blah oos-TEHD een-GLEHS] = Do you speak English? (formal)
- ¿Hablas inglés? [AH-blahs een-GLEHS] = Do you speak English? (informal)
- ¿Hay alguien que hable inglés? [AY AHL-gyehn keh AH-bleh een-GLEHS] = Is there someone here who speaks English?
- No hablo bien español. [noh AH-bloh BYEHN ehs-pah-NYOHL] = I can't speak Spanish well.
- Entiendo. [ehn-TYEHN-doh] = I understand.
- No entiendo. [noh ehn-TYEHN-doh] = I don't understand.
- ¿Puede hablar más despacio, por favor? [PWEH-deh ah-BLAR mahs dehs-PAH-thyoh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Can you speak more slowly, please?
- ¿Podría usted repetirlo, por favor? [poh-DREE-ah oos-TEHD reh-peh-TEER-loh, pohr fah-BOHR] = Could you repeat that, please?
- ¿Cómo se dice ... en español? [KOH-moh seh DEE-theh ... ehn ehs-pah-NYOHL] = How do you say ... in Spanish?
- Oiga. [OY-gah] = Listen / excuse me (to get someone's attention).

## Numbers
Country: ES (Spain)

- cero = 0; uno = 1; dos = 2; tres = 3; cuatro = 4; cinco = 5; seis = 6; siete = 7; ocho = 8; nueve = 9; diez = 10
- once = 11; doce = 12; trece = 13; catorce = 14; quince = 15; dieciséis = 16; diecisiete = 17; dieciocho = 18; diecinueve = 19; veinte = 20
- veintiuno = 21; veintidós = 22; veinticinco = 25; treinta = 30; treinta y uno = 31; cuarenta = 40; cincuenta = 50; sesenta = 60; setenta = 70; ochenta = 80; noventa = 90
- cien = 100; ciento veinticinco = 125; doscientos = 200; trescientos = 300; quinientos = 500; setecientos = 700; novecientos = 900; mil = 1,000; dos mil = 2,000
  Tens and units are joined with y from 31 to 99 (treinta y dos = 32). Before a feminine noun, the hundreds become -as: doscientas pesetas, but doscientos kilos. Spain writes thousands with a dot (1.000) and decimals with a comma. Years are said as full numbers: 1984 = mil novecientos ochenta y cuatro.

## Days, weeks and months
Country: ALL (any Spanish-speaking country)

- lunes = Monday; martes = Tuesday; miércoles = Wednesday; jueves = Thursday; viernes = Friday; sábado = Saturday; domingo = Sunday
- el lunes [ehl LOO-nehs] = on Monday
- hoy [OY] = today; ayer [ah-YEHR] = yesterday; mañana [mah-NYAH-nah] = tomorrow
- mañana por la mañana [mah-NYAH-nah pohr lah mah-NYAH-nah] = tomorrow morning
- el jueves por la tarde [ehl HWEH-behs pohr lah TAR-deh] = on Thursday afternoon
- esta noche [EHS-tah NOH-cheh] = tonight
- este fin de semana [EHS-teh feen deh seh-MAH-nah] = this weekend
- la próxima semana [lah PROHK-see-mah seh-MAH-nah] = next week
- una hora = an hour; un día = a day; una semana = a week; un mes = a month; un año = a year
- enero = January; febrero = February; marzo = March; abril = April; mayo = May; junio = June; julio = July; agosto = August; septiembre = September; octubre = October; noviembre = November; diciembre = December
  Days and months are written with a small letter in Spanish.

## Telling the time
Country: ES (Spain)

- ¿Qué hora es? [keh OH-rah ehs] = What time is it?
- Es la una. [ehs lah OO-nah] = It's one o'clock.
- Son las tres. [sohn lahs trehs] = It's three o'clock.
- Son las siete y diez. [sohn lahs SYEH-teh ee dyehth] = It's ten past seven.
- Son las doce y cuarto. [sohn lahs DOH-theh ee KWAR-toh] = It's a quarter past twelve.
- Es la una y media. [ehs lah OO-nah ee MEH-dyah] = It's half past one.
- Son las nueve menos cuarto. [sohn lahs NWEH-beh MEH-nohs KWAR-toh] = It's a quarter to nine.
- a las siete [ah lahs SYEH-teh] = at seven o'clock
  More on clock times and dates: see hotel_and_time.md.

## Greetings and introductions, formal and informal (Latin America)
Country: 419 (Unspecified Latin America)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Latin America Headstart Course", Module I Unit 1 "Greetings and Introductions" (pp. 12-14, 20; dialogues set in Panama), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-latin-america. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- Le presento a mi esposa Gloria. [leh preh-SEHN-toh ah mee ehs-POH-sah GLOH-ryah] = May I introduce my wife Gloria. (formal: le = to you)
- Mucho gusto, señora de Miller. [MOO-choh GOOS-toh, seh-NYOH-rah deh MEE-lehr] = Glad to meet you, Mrs. Miller. (A married woman is "señora de" + her husband's family name.)
- Encantada. [ehn-kahn-TAH-dah] = Delighted (to meet you). (said only by women)
- A sus órdenes. [ah soos OHR-deh-nehs] = At your service.
  Mucho gusto can also mean "a pleasure to have met you" when you say goodbye to someone you have just met.
- ¡Hola, Ricardo! ¿Cómo estás? [OH-lah, rree-KAHR-doh. KOH-moh ehs-TAHS] = Hi, Ricardo! How are you? (informal, to a friend)
- Ahí, pasándola. [ah-EE, pah-SAHN-doh-lah] = Getting by.
- ¿Y tú? [ee TOO] = And you? (informal)
- Regular. [rreh-goo-LAHR] = So-so.
- Jorge, te presento a mi amiga Virginia López. [HOHR-heh, teh preh-SEHN-toh ah mee ah-MEE-gah beer-HEE-nyah LOH-pehs] = Jorge, this is my friend Virginia López. (informal: te = to you)

## Meeting people (Puerto Rico)
Country: PR (Puerto Rico)

Source: Defense Language Institute, "Spanish Headstart for Puerto Rico", Module I Unit 1 "Greetings and Introductions" (p. 13), Unit 2 "In Puerto Rico" (p. 23), Unit 3 "Where Do You Live?" (p. 37) and the keys (pp. 205-207), https://www.livelingua.com/course/fsi/spanish-headstart-for-puerto-rico. US Government work (DLI); no license statement in the PDF. English glosses and pronunciations are ours.

- Muy bien, gracias. ¿Y usted? [MOO-ee BYEHN, GRAH-syahs. ee oos-TEHD] = Very well, thank you. And you?
- Señor Aponte, le presento a mi esposa Lisa. [seh-NYOR ah-POHN-teh, leh preh-SEHN-toh ah mee ehs-POH-sah LEE-sah] = Mr. Aponte, let me introduce my wife, Lisa.
- Mucho gusto, señora. [MOO-choh GOOS-toh, seh-NYOH-rah] = Pleased to meet you, ma'am.
- Encantada, señor. [ehn-kahn-TAH-dah, seh-NYOR] = Delighted, sir. (a woman says it; a man says Encantado)
- ¿Cuánto tiempo hace que vive usted en Puerto Rico? [KWAHN-toh TYEHM-poh AH-seh keh BEE-beh oos-TEHD ehn PWEHR-toh REE-koh] = How long have you been living in Puerto Rico?
- Hace dos meses que vivo aquí. [AH-seh dohs MEH-sehs keh BEE-boh ah-KEE] = I've been living here for two months.
- ¿Le gusta Puerto Rico? [leh GOOS-tah PWEHR-toh REE-koh] = Do you like Puerto Rico?
- Sí, me gusta, pero hace mucho calor. [SEE, meh GOOS-tah, PEH-roh AH-seh MOO-choh kah-LOR] = Yes, I like it, but it's very hot.
- ¿De dónde son ustedes? [deh DOHN-deh sohn oos-TEH-dehs] = Where are you from? (asking more than one person)
- Somos de California. [SOH-mohs deh kah-lee-FOR-nyah] = We are from California.
- ¿Tienen ustedes hijos? [TYEH-nehn oos-TEH-dehs EE-hohs] = Do you have children?
- Sí, tenemos dos hijos, un niño y una niña. [SEE, teh-NEH-mohs dohs EE-hohs, oon NEE-nyoh ee OO-nah NEE-nyah] = Yes, we have two children, a boy and a girl.
- ¿Dónde viven ustedes? [DOHN-deh BEE-behn oos-TEH-dehs] = Where do you live? (to more than one person)
- Lo siento, amigos, pero tengo que irme. [loh SYEHN-toh, ah-MEE-gohs, PEH-roh TEHN-goh keh EER-meh] = I'm sorry, friends, but I have to go.
- Hasta la vista. [AHS-tah lah BEES-tah] = See you. / So long.
  Mucho gusto can also mean "a pleasure to have met you" when you say goodbye to someone you have just met.

## Informal greetings with vos (Paraguay)
Country: PY (Paraguay)

Source: Peace Corps, "Spanish I and Work Book (For Peace Corps Volunteers)", Unidad Uno (p. 7) and Unidad Cuatro/Cinco dialogues (pp. 46, 53), https://www.livelingua.com/course/peace-corps/spanish-language-lessons---paraguay. Peace Corps (US Government) training material; the ERIC copy states it is federally funded or otherwise in the public domain. English glosses are the book's where it gives one, otherwise ours; pronunciations are ours (Latin American: z/ce/ci = "s", ll/y = "y").

- Hola, ¿qué tal? [OH-lah, keh TAHL] = Hello, how are you doing?
- ¿Cómo te va? [KOH-moh teh BAH] = How is it going?
- ¿Cómo andás? [KOH-moh ahn-DAHS] = How is it going? (vos form)
- Bien, ¿y vos? [BYEHN, ee BOHS] = Fine, and you? (vos = informal "you" in Paraguay)
- Al pelo. [ahl PEH-loh] = Great!
- Bien, y vos, ¿qué contás? [BYEHN, ee bohs, keh kohn-TAHS] = Fine, and you, what's new?
- Tanto tiempo sin verte. [TAHN-toh TYEHM-poh seen BEHR-teh] = Long time no see.
- Listo, chau. [LEES-toh, CHOW] = OK, bye.
- Hasta la vista. [AHS-tah lah BEES-tah] = See you.
- Saludos a tu familia. [sah-LOO-dohs ah too fah-MEE-lyah] = Say hello to your family.
- Gracias, igualmente. [GRAH-syahs, ee-gwahl-MEHN-teh] = Thanks, same to you.
  With friends Paraguayans use vos instead of tú, with its own verb forms: vos sos (you are), vos tenés (you have), ¿cómo te llamás? (what's your name?).

## Greetings, goodbyes and introductions (Uruguay)
Country: UY (Uruguay)

Source: Peace Corps Uruguay, "Español I: Manual (para Peace Corps Volunteers)" (1993), Unidad 1 (pp. 2-3) and Unidad 2 (p. 17), https://www.livelingua.com/course/peace-corps/uruguay-spanish-manual. Peace Corps (US Government) training material; ERIC ED 402 766. English glosses are the manual's unless marked; pronunciations are ours (Uruguay: z/ce/ci = "s", ll/y = "sh").

- Hola, ¿qué tal? [OH-lah, keh TAHL] = Hello, how are you doing?
- ¿Cómo te va? [KOH-moh teh BAH] = How is it going? (informal)
- ¿Cómo andás? [KOH-moh ahn-DAHS] = How is it going? (informal, vos form)
- ¡Bárbaro! [BAR-bah-roh] = It's great!
- Muy bien, también. [MOO-ee BYEHN, tahm-BYEHN] = Very well, too.
- Chau. [CHOW] = Bye.
- Hasta pronto. [AHS-tah PROHN-toh] = I'll see you soon.
- Hasta mañana. [AHS-tah mah-NYAH-nah] = I'll see you tomorrow.
- Quiero presentarle a ... [KYEH-roh preh-sehn-TAR-leh ah] = I want to introduce you to ... (formal)
- Te presento a mi amigo José. [teh preh-SEHN-toh ah mee ah-MEE-goh hoh-SEH] = This is my friend José. (informal)
- El placer es mío. [ehl plah-SEHR ehs MEE-oh] = The pleasure is mine.
- Vos sos ... [bohs SOHS] = You are ... (vos form of ser; the manual lists soy, sos, es, somos, son. Gloss ours.)
  Usted is for someone older than you, a stranger, someone you have just met, or someone in authority. Tú and vos are for people your own age or younger, relatives and close friends. When in doubt, use usted, or wait for the Uruguayan speaker to set the pattern (summarised from the manual, p. 17).
