# Macedonian for shopping and money

Source: Daniela Jankovska, "Tips & Guide to: Learn Macedonian Language" (native speaker). https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5795274dc534a514ed1a2672/t/5ff8c0fa576d293b16b788fe/1610137853068/Learn+Macedonian+Language+By+Daniela+Jankovska.pdf. Spelling typos in the source corrected.

## Asking about products and prices

- Дали имате ...? [Dali imate ...?] [DA-lee EE-ma-te ...?] = Do you have ...?
- Колку чини ова? / Колку пари е ова? [Kolku chini ova? / Kolku pari e ova?] [KOL-koo CHEE-nee O-va? / KOL-koo PA-ree e O-va?] = How much is this?
- Го имате ова во мој број? [Go imate ova vo moj broj?] [go EE-ma-te O-va vo MOY BROY?] = Do you have this in my size?
- Ми треба ... [Mi treba ...] [MEE tre-ba ...] = I need ...
- Барам ... [Baram ...] [BA-ram ...] = I'm looking for ...

## Deciding to buy (or not)

- Тоа е скапо. [Toa e skapo.] [TO-a e SKA-po.] = That's expensive.
- Тоа е ефтино. [Toa e eftino.] [TO-a e EF-tee-no.] = That's cheap.
- Извинете, немам пари. [Izvinete, nemam pari.] [eez-VEE-ne-te, NE-mam PA-ree.] = Sorry, I can't afford it (literally "I have no money").
- Не сум заинтересиран. (m.) / Не сум заинтересирана. (f.) [Ne sum zainteresiran. / Ne sum zainteresirana.] [NE soom za-een-te-RE-see-ran. / NE soom za-een-te-re-SEE-ra-na.] = I'm not interested.
- Добро, ќе го земам. [Dobro, kje go zemam.] [DO-bro, kye GO ze-mam.] = OK, I'll take it.
- Може ли кеса? [Mozhe li kesa?] [MO-zhe lee KE-sa?] = Can I have a bag?

## Shops and what to buy there

- продавница [prodavnica] [pro-DAV-nee-tsa] = shop / grocery store; аптека [apteka] [AP-te-ka] = pharmacy
- паста за заби / четка за заби [pasta za zabi / chetka za zabi] [PAS-ta za ZA-bee / CHET-ka za ZA-bee] = toothpaste / toothbrush; шампон [shampon] [SHAM-pon] = shampoo
- апче [apche] [AP-che] = pill; Парацетамол [Paracetamol] [pa-ra-TSE-ta-mol] = paracetamol (pain medicine); апче за стомак [apche za stomak] [AP-che za STO-mak] = stomach medicine
- мајца / блуза [majca / bluza] [MAY-tsa / BLOO-za] = T-shirt / blouse; шорцеви / панталони [shorcevi / pantaloni] [SHOR-tse-vee / pan-TA-lo-nee] = shorts / trousers
- фустан / јакна [fustan / jakna] [FOOS-tan / YAK-na] = dress / jacket; чевли / сандали [chevli / sandali] [CHE-vlee / SAN-da-lee] = shoes / sandals (кондури is a colloquial word for shoes); марама / шал [marama / shal] [MA-ra-ma / SHAL] = scarf

## Money and paying

- пари [pari] [PA-ree] = money; денар (мн. денари) [denar (mn. denari)] [DE-nar (MN. DE-na-ree)] = denar, the Macedonian currency; евро [evro] [E-vro] = euro
- кредитна картичка [kreditna kartichka] [KRE-deet-na KAR-teech-ka] = credit card
- Сметката, ве молам. [Smetkata, ve molam.] [SMET-ka-ta, VE mo-lam.] = The bill / receipt, please.
- менувачница [menuvachnica] [me-noo-VACH-nee-tsa] = currency exchange office
- Ми треба менувачница за размена на пари. [Mi treba menuvachnica za razmena na pari.] [MEE tre-ba me-noo-VACH-nee-tsa za RAZ-me-na na PA-ree.] = I need an exchange office to change money.
- Banknotes say "Народна банка на Република Македонија" and come in 10, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000 and 5000 denars (десет, педесет, сто ... денари).
- Exchange rates change; check a current rate rather than relying on a fixed number. The guide's advice: if something costs more than about $300 (clothes, food, tours), it is probably overpriced for Macedonia, so ask a local.

## Market and souvenirs: Useful phrases
- Колку чини ова? [Kolku chini ova?] [KOL-koo CHEE-nee O-va?] = How much is this?
- Колку чини килото? [Kolku chini kiloto?] [KOL-koo CHEE-nee KEE-lo-to?] = How much is a kilo?
- Дајте ми едно кило домати, ве молам. [Dajte mi edno kilo domati, ve molam.] [DAY-te mee ED-no KEE-lo DO-ma-tee, VE mo-lam.] = Give me a kilo of tomatoes, please.
- Само гледам, благодарам. [Samo gledam, blagodaram.] [SA-mo GLE-dam, bla-GO-da-ram.] = I'm just looking, thank you.
- Може ли малку поефтино? [Mozhe li malku poeftino?] [MO-zhe lee MAL-koo po-EF-tee-no?] = Can it be a bit cheaper?
- Ќе го земам. [Kje go zemam.] [kye GO ze-mam.] = I'll take it.
- Имате ли кесе? [Imate li kese?] [ee-MA-te lee KE-se?] = Do you have a bag?
- Може ли со картичка? [Mozhe li so kartichka?] [MO-zhe lee so KAR-teech-ka?] = Can I pay by card?

## Market and souvenirs: Sample dialogue: green market
- Продавачката: Повелете, свежи домати! [Povelete, svezhi domati!] [po-VE-le-te, SVE-zhee DO-ma-tee!] = Seller (f): Here you go, fresh tomatoes!
- Колку чини килото? [Kolku chini kiloto?] [KOL-koo CHEE-nee KEE-lo-to?] = How much is a kilo?
- Продавачката: Осумдесет денари. [Osumdeset denari.] [o-SOOM-de-set DE-na-ree.] = Seller: Eighty denars.
- Дајте ми едно кило, ве молам. И половина кило пиперки. [Dajte mi edno kilo, ve molam. I polovina kilo piperki.] [DAY-te mee ED-no KEE-lo, VE mo-lam. ee po-LO-vee-na KEE-lo PEE-per-kee.] = Give me one kilo, please. And half a kilo of peppers.
- Продавачката: Нешто друго? [Neshto drugo?] [NESH-to DROO-go?] = Seller: Anything else?
- Не, тоа е сè. [Ne, toa e se.] [NE, TO-a e Sè.] = No, that's all.
- Продавачката: Сто и дваесет денари вкупно. [Sto i dvaeset denari vkupno.] [STO ee DVA-e-set DE-na-ree VKOOP-no.] = Seller: One hundred and twenty denars in total.

## Market and souvenirs: Sample dialogue: souvenir shop in the Old Bazaar
- Добар ден. Колку чини овој прстен? [Dobar den. Kolku chini ovoj prsten?] [DO-bar DEN. KOL-koo CHEE-nee O-voy PRS-ten?] = Hello. How much is this ring?
- Продавачот: Тоа е филигран, рачна изработка. Две илјади денари. [Toa e filigran, rachna izrabotka. Dve iljadi denari.] [TO-a e FEE-lee-gran, RACH-na ee-ZRA-bot-ka. DVE EEL-ya-dee DE-na-ree.] = Seller: It's filigree, handmade. Two thousand denars.
- Малку е скапо. Може ли поефтино? [Malku e skapo. Mozhe li poeftino?] [MAL-koo e SKA-po. MO-zhe lee po-EF-tee-no?] = It's a bit expensive. Can it be cheaper?
- Продавачот: За вас, илјада и осумстотини. [Za vas, iljada i osumstotini.] [za VAS, EEL-ya-da ee o-soom-STO-tee-nee.] = Seller: For you, one thousand eight hundred.
- Добро, ќе го земам. [Dobro, kje go zemam.] [DO-bro, kye GO ze-mam.] = OK, I'll take it.

## Market and souvenirs: Culture notes
Green markets (Bit Pazar in Skopje is the biggest) sell fresh, cheap local produce, and prices are per kilo. Small shops and markets often prefer cash in denars. Polite bargaining is normal for souvenirs in the Old Bazaar, but not in supermarkets or regular shops. Prices in the dialogues are examples only.
