# Macedonian at a restaurant or café

Source: Peace Corps Macedonia, "Macedonian Language / Makedonski Jazik", Lesson 7 (Expressions) and Lesson 3 (Numbers).
https://www.fsi-language-courses.org/macedonian/courses/peace-corps-macedonian-language-lessons/

## Getting attention and being served
- Добар ден! [Dobar den!] [DO-bar DEN!] = Good day! (polite greeting when you walk in)
- Извинете. [Izvinete.] [eez-VEE-ne-te.] = Excuse me. (to call the waiter)
- Повелете. [Povelete.] [po-VE-le-te.] = Here you are. / May I help you? (the waiter will often say this to you)
- Благодарам. [Blagodaram.] [bla-GO-da-ram.] = Thank you.

## Asking about food and prices
- Што е ова? [Shto e ova?] [SHTO e O-va?] = What is this?
- Колку чини? [Kolku chini?] [KOL-koo CHEE-nee?] = How much is it?
- Како се вика ... на македонски? [Kako se vika ... na makedonski?] [KA-ko SE vee-ka ... na ma-KE-don-skee?] = How do you say ... in Macedonian? (useful for coffee, water, etc.)
- вода [voda] [VO-da] = water.  храна [hrana] [HRA-na] = food.

## Dietary needs and how you feel
Macedonian adjectives change with the speaker's gender: (m.) = a man says it, (f.) = a woman says it.
- Јас сум вегетаријанец. (m.) / Јас сум вегетаријанка. (f.) [Jas sum vegetarijanec. / Jas sum vegetarijanka.] [YAS soom ve-ge-ta-REE-ya-nets. / YAS soom ve-ge-ta-REE-yan-ka.] = I am a vegetarian.
- Гладен сум. (m.) / Гладна сум. (f.) [Gladen sum. / Gladna sum.] [GLA-den soom. / GLAD-na soom.] = I'm hungry.
- Сит сум. (m.) / Сита сум. (f.) [Sit sum. / Sita sum.] [SEET soom. / SEE-ta soom.] = I'm full.

## Toasting
- На здравје! [Na zdravje!] [na ZDRAV-ye!] = Cheers! (The same phrase also means "Bless you" after a sneeze.)

## Understanding prices
Tens and units are joined with и ("and"): дваесет и пет = 25. Useful round numbers: педесет 50, сто 100, илјада 1000.
If the waiter speaks too fast: Ве молам, зборувајте побавно. [Ve molam, zboruvajte pobavno.] = Please, speak slowly.

## Ordering food
- Сакам ... [Sakam ...] [SA-kam ...] = I want ...
- Сакам храна која содржи пилешко / говедско / риба / колбас / салата. [Sakam hrana koja sodrzhi pileshko / govedsko / riba / kolbas / salata.] [SA-kam HRA-na KO-ya SO-dr-zhee PEE-lesh-ko / GO-ved-sko / REE-ba / KOL-bas / SA-la-ta.] = I want a dish with chicken / beef / fish / sausage / salad.
- Јас не јадам свинско / говедско месо. [Jas ne jadam svinsko / govedsko meso.] [YAS NE ya-dam SVEEN-sko / GO-ved-sko ME-so.] = I don't eat pork / beef.
- Луто. [Luto.] [LOO-to.] = Spicy.  Не сакам луто. [Ne sakam luto.] [NE sa-kam LOO-to.] = I don't want it spicy.
- Ми треба друга лажица / виљушка / чаша. [Mi treba druga lazhica / viljushka / chasha.] [MEE tre-ba DROO-ga LA-zhee-tsa / VEE-lyoosh-ka / CHA-sha.] = I need another spoon / fork / glass.

## Ordering drinks
- Може една чаша вода / сок / кафе / чај? [Mozhe edna chasha voda / sok / kafe / chaj?] [MO-zhe ED-na CHA-sha VO-da / SOK / KA-fe / CHAY?] = May I have a glass of water / juice / coffee / tea?
- Може една чаша бело вино / црвено вино? [Mozhe edna chasha belo vino / crveno vino?] [MO-zhe ED-na CHA-sha BE-lo VEE-no / TSR-ve-no VEE-no?] = May I have a glass of white wine / red wine?
- Едно пиво, ве молам. [Edno pivo, ve molam.] [ED-no PEE-vo, VE mo-lam.] = A beer, please.
- Друга тура! [Druga tura!] [DROO-ga TOO-ra!] = Another round!
- Вода со лед [Voda so led] [VO-da so LED] = water with ice.  Без лед [Bez led] [bez LED] = no ice.
- Кисела вода [Kisela voda] [KEE-se-la VO-da] = sparkling (mineral) water.
- Ракија [Rakija] [RA-kee-ya] = local brandy.  Пиво [Pivo] [PEE-vo] = beer.  Кафе [Kafe] [KA-fe] = coffee.  Чај [Chaj] [CHAY] = tea.

## Paying
- Многу беше вкусно. Сметката, ве молам. [Mnogu beshe vkusno. Smetkata, ve molam.] [MNO-goo BE-she VKOOS-no. SMET-ka-ta, VE mo-lam.] = It was delicious. The check, please.
- Сметката, ве молам. [Smetkata, ve molam.] [SMET-ka-ta, VE mo-lam.] = The bill, please.
- Пари [Pari] [PA-ree] = money.  Кредитна картичка [Kreditna kartichka] [KRE-deet-na KAR-teech-ka] = credit card.

## What the waiter asks you

- Што ќе порачате? [Shto kje porachate?] [SHTO kye po-RA-cha-te?] = What will you order?

## A typical Macedonian menu (Kramer)
Based on the sample menu of the restaurant "Македонска куќа" in the textbook.
- Предјадења (starters): бело сирење, печени пиперки, пита со месо, пита со сирење, пита со месо и печурки
- Супи и чорби (soups): јагнешка чорба, пилешка супа, говедска супа
- Риби (fish): бела риба, пастрмка на охридски начин (Ohrid-style trout), печен крап (baked carp)
- Месо (meat): печено јагнешко месо, печено свинско месо, печено пилешко месо, селско месо, ќофте
- Јадења на скара (grilled): мешано месо на скара (mixed grill), свински кременадли (pork chops), плескавица, ќебапчиња
- Салати: шопска салата, туршија, салата од кисела зелка, салата од домати, салата од краставици
- Слатки (desserts): баклава, кадаиф, овошје, сладолед
- Безалкохолни пијалоци (soft drinks): кисела вода, Кока-кола, сок од јаболка, чај, турско кафе
- Алкохолни пијалоци: бело и црно вино (note: red wine is "црно вино", literally black wine), пиво

## Food culture notes (Kramer)
- Meat types are adjectives in the neuter, modifying месо: свинско (pork), јагнешко (lamb), пилешко (chicken), говедско (beef). You can say just "свинско" for pork.
- Пита is a phyllo-like dough stuffed with fillings; бурек is similar and sold at take-out stands.
- Супа is a soup with a water-based broth; чорба is a thick soup with a cream- or milk-based broth and more pieces of meat and vegetables.
- Сирење means cheese; the most common is бело сирење (white, feta-style cheese). Кашкавал is a yellow sheep's-milk cheese; restaurants often serve поховано кашкавал (breaded and fried).
- Шопска салата: chopped tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, onion (кромид) and grated feta cheese.
- Peppers are either лут (hot) or благ (mild/sweet). Лут also means "angry": Тој е лут = He is angry.
- Many Macedonians say патлиџан for tomato in speech; the standard written word (on menus and in recipes) is домат. Eggplant is модар (or црн) патлиџан.
- Кисело млеко is thick yogurt eaten with a spoon; јогурт is a drinkable, liquid yogurt.
- Селско месо: a casserole of meat chunks with onions, peppers, carrots and mushrooms. Кадаиф: a pastry of shredded dough in sweet syrup. Баклава: phyllo pastry with nuts and honey.
- Печен (roasted), пржен (fried) and полнет (stuffed) agree with the noun: печени пиперки, пржени пиперки, пржено јајце, полнети пиперки.

## Restaurant basics: Sample dialogue
- Келнерот: Добар ден! Повелете, што би сакале да нарачате? [Dobar den! Povelete, shto bi sakale da narachate?] [DO-bar DEN! po-VE-le-te, SHTO bee SA-ka-le da na-RA-cha-te?] = Waiter: Good afternoon! What would you like to order?
- Добар ден. Би сакал тавче гравче и една шопска салата. [Dobar den. Bi sakal tavche gravche i edna shopska salata.] [DO-bar DEN. BEE sa-kal TAV-che GRAV-che ee ED-na SHOP-ska SA-la-ta.] = Hello. I would like tavche gravche and a shopska salad.
- Келнерот: Нешто за пиење? [Neshto za pienje?] [NESH-to za PEE-e-nye?] = Waiter: Anything to drink?
- Една чаша црвено вино, ве молам. [Edna chasha crveno vino, ve molam.] [ED-na CHA-sha TSR-ve-no VEE-no, VE mo-lam.] = A glass of red wine, please.
- Келнерот: Веднаш. Пријатно! [Vednash. Prijatno!] [VED-nash. PREE-yat-no!] = Waiter: Right away. Enjoy your meal!
- Сметката, ве молам. [Smetkata, ve molam.] [SMET-ka-ta, VE mo-lam.] = The bill, please.

## Restaurant basics: Culture notes
Use the polite form (Вие, "ve molam") with waiters and staff. "Пријатно" is said when food is served (enjoy your meal) and also as a polite goodbye. Leaving about 10% or rounding up the bill is a common, friendly tip but not required. Lunch is often the main meal of the day, and traditional restaurants (кафана, kafana) serve grilled meat, salads and rakija.

## Kafana: Culture notes
A kafana is a traditional restaurant, often with live music in the evening. People order salads and rakija first and the main dish later, and meals are slow and social. Portions are big, so it is normal to share. "Повелете пак" (come again) is a common goodbye from staff.

## Café: Culture notes
Going for coffee ("на кафе") is a big part of social life: people sit for an hour or more with one coffee, and nobody will rush you. The macchiato is the default coffee in most cafés. Turkish coffee is more common at home and in traditional places in the Old Bazaar. You usually pay at the table when you are ready to leave.

## Local dishes: Main dishes
- тавче гравче [tavche gravche] [TAV-che GRAV-che] = baked beans in a clay pot with onion and red pepper; the national dish, traditionally a meatless (fasting) meal
- селско месо [selsko meso] [SEL-sko ME-so] = "village meat": pork or veal cooked with mushrooms, peppers and wine, served in a clay pot
- сарма [sarma] [SAR-ma] = cabbage or vine leaves stuffed with minced meat and rice
- полнети пиперки [polneti piperki] [POL-ne-tee PEE-per-kee] = peppers stuffed with minced meat and rice (or with beans, as a fasting dish)
- мусака [musaka] [MOO-sa-ka] = baked layers of potatoes and minced meat
- кебапчиња [kebapchinja] [ke-BAP-chee-nya] = small grilled minced-meat rolls, served with chopped onion and bread

- турли тава [turli tava] [TOOR-lee TA-va] = mixed vegetables (and sometimes meat) baked in a clay dish

## Local dishes: Culture notes
Bread is served with almost every meal, and salads with rakija come before the main dish. Many dishes are baked in clay pots (тава / grne). On religious fasting days people eat meatless versions of the same dishes, so restaurants usually have vegetarian options such as tavche gravche or stuffed peppers with beans. In autumn families make "зимница" (winter preserves) like ajvar and pickles at home. If you are invited into someone's home, expect to be offered coffee, slatko, or rakija; it's polite to accept at least a little.
